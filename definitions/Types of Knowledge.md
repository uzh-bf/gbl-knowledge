There are several types of knowledge recognized in educational psychology and epistemology. Commonly identified types are:
## Factual Knowledge:
This type of knowledge involves information that is specific, concrete, and verifiable. It includes facts and data that form the building block of understanding within that field. It can be described as the foundational and essential information that students need to be familiar with a particular subject. Factual knowledge provides the necessary groundwork for further comprehension, problem-solving, and higher-level thinking within a given domain.

Example: The capital of France is Paris.
Explanation: This is a straightforward information and can be stated as a fact. It is about knowing specific details.
## Declarative Knowledge:
This category goes beyond individual facts and involves understanding the relationships between dif-ferent pieces of information. It focuses on the organization and structure of knowledge.

Example: Understanding the concept of gravity and how it influences the motion of objects.
Explanation: Here, it is about understanding relationship between mass, attraction, and the effect of gravity on objects.
## Procedural Knowledge:
This is about knowing how to do something. It involves processes, methods, and techniques to perform specific tasks or to be specific, solving a problem. Unlike factual knowledge, which deals with what is true or known, procedural knowledge focuses on the “how” aspect of skills and actions.

Example: Knowledge about how to ride a bicycle
Explanation: This involves practical know-how and the ability to execute a series of steps or procedures to achieve a specific task.

## Metacognitive Knowledge:

Metacognitive knowledge is awareness and understanding of one’s own cognitive processes. It includes knowledge about how to plan, monitor and regulate one’s own thinking and learning. It is crucial for effective self-regulation of learning.

Example: You’re having difficulty understanding a concept or an financial model and deciding to seek additional resources.
Explanation: Goes beyond the specific of a subject and involves understanding your own thinking processes – being aware of your strengths and weaknesses in learning and taking proactive steps to enhance your understanding.
