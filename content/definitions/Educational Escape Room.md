## Introduction

Escape rooms are commonly perceived as recreational activities, synonymous with enjoyment and playfulness. However, their potential for educational purposes is often overlooked by many. Through game simulations students can recognise their strengths and weaknesses, increase their motivation to learn and receive instant feedback to improve their knowledge ([[Mawhirter and Garofalo (2016)]]). The first documented escape game was created in Japan in 2007 ([[Nicholson (2015)]]). “Escape rooms are live-action team-based games where players discover clues, solve puzzles, and accomplish tasks in one or more rooms in order to accomplish a specific goal (usually escaping from the room) in a limited amount of time” ([[Nicholson (2015)]]). The development of educational escape rooms began in 2015 with enthusiastic teachers who made their materials available on platforms such as Breakout EDU ([[Rouse (2017)]]). In recent years, the concept of educational escape rooms has gained considerable traction within academic circles ([[Lopez-Pernas et al. (2019a)]]). Since escape rooms bear resemblances to educational games, we can draw upon theories of game-based learning ([[Veldkamp et al. (2020)]]). Game-based learning is an active teaching method that utilises structures and rules of games to teach learning content (Helbing et al. (2022)). Students can explore and apply new concepts and skills in a safe environment (Helbing et al. (2022)). Active learning, in contrast to passive learning, where students only listen and memorise the learning content, includes a wide range of pedagogical measures that require the active participation of students in the classroom ([[Bonwell and Eison (1991)]]). Most studies examining the impact of educational games have reported that game-based learning enhances knowledge acquisition, mastery of content, and motivation ([[Connolly et al. (2012)]] and [[Subhash and Cudney (2018)]]).

Several studies have reported on the use of educational escape rooms in a variety of disciplines such as in medicine and STEM (science, technology, engineering, and mathematics) education ([[Veldkamp et al. (2020)]]), but according to the author’s research, there are fewer studies discussing the use of educational escape rooms in business and finance.

## Impact and outcomes of educational escape rooms

In the 21st century learning is a hybrid process where educational experiences and activities occur in both digital and physical contexts ([[Clarke et al. (2017)]]). Nowadays, students think and process information differently than in the past, making it challenging for teachers to motivate and engage them with conventional learning methods ([[Fotaris and Mastoras (2019)]]). Gamification is one way to promote active learning in the classroom ([[Brady and Andersen (2021)]]). By combining active learning and gamification, students have the opportunity to learn new concepts, review concepts covered in class, and it also helps students see where they stand with their knowledge level ([[Bord (2008)]]). Students who participated in gamified learning courses demonstrated higher attendance rates and showed a greater interest in learning ([[Barata et al. (2013)]]). Students find learning through play more exciting than traditional methods ([[Fotaris and Mastoras (2019)]]). Escape rooms provide students with an opportunity to engage in active, hands-on learning, allowing them to practice both hard and soft skills ([[Clarke et al. (2017)]]). When students are actively involved in the learning process then they can better retain and comprehend the material ([[Brady and Andersen (2021)]]). Learner-centred teaching approaches incorporate active, cooperative, and problem-oriented learning techniques ([[Prince (2004)]]). These approaches provide students with the opportunity to better understand the application of a topic through critical thinking ([[Brady and Andersen (2021)]]). “Active learning is a socio-constructivist theory that advocates discussing content in the context of learners' prior experiences and social interaction” ([[Guckian, Sridhar, and Meggitt (2020)]]). “It promotes active tasks such as writing, discussion, and reflection to facilitate learning” ([[Guckian, Sridhar, and Meggitt (2020)]]). Escape rooms are usually worked in groups and are therefore a suitable environment for collaborative learning ([[Lopez-Pernas et al. (2019a)]]). Many students reported learning from their team members as well ([[Guckian, Sridhar, and Meggitt (2020)]]). Additionally, escape rooms can provide students with insight into how tasks might look in their future careers ([[Guckian, Sridhar, and Meggitt (2020)]]). For many students, escape rooms are a cool experience that boost their confidence in the topics covered ([[Guckian, Sridhar, and Meggitt (2020)]]).

After the escape room activity, students have a deeper understanding of the topics and feel more confident answering questions in these areas. For many students, the escape room activity helps to see the learned concepts within a broader context. Additionally, escape rooms are an excellent way to review material for exams. They increase student engagement, improve retention and enhance teamwork. Through escape rooms, students have the opportunity to practice efficient problem-solving, and the skills learned can often be transferred to other situations. In an escape room, students collaborate to achieve the same objective, placing them at the centre of the learning process. Learner-centred practices, like escape rooms, foster a deeper comprehension of concepts and provide a more effective and enjoyable way to prepare for exams than typical review sessions. Escape rooms encourage students to learn from one another's thought processes and boost their confidence to talk about complex themes ([[Brady and Andersen (2021)]]).

Collaborating as a team under time pressure enables students to test and enhance their communication skills ([[Fotaris and Mastoras (2019)]]). Escape rooms also mitigate the free-rider problem, ensuring that all students must actively participate to solve the tasks efficiently ([[Fotaris and Mastoras (2019)]]). Escape rooms also encourage students to view problems from multiple perspectives, promote teamwork, enhance engagement and perseverance in task completion, and foster deeper understanding through group discussions ([[Fotaris and Mastoras (2019)]]). During the escape room activity, students are more engaged in problem-solving and have more fun compared to a regular class. In a study all students were active in solving the tasks. The study shows that some groups experienced conflicts during the activity due to disagreements on how to proceed, while in other groups, a few students dominated while others merely kept silent. The most effective and efficient groups were those that assigned different roles to each student at the beginning of the activity, such as note-taking or calculations, and had a single leader to coordinate the team instead of multiple leaders or no leader ([[Cain (2019)]]). In a different study, the group dynamics during an escape room were analysed and it was found that groups consisting of friends and strangers were most positively influenced by the escape room activity with respect to values and behaviour than groups consisting only of friends or only of strangers ([[Chang (2019)]]).

Many students report that escape rooms activities encourage them to learn, help them to memorise and categorise the material better, and also show them which topics they should focus on more ([[Gómez-Urquiza et al. (2019)]], [[Morrell and Ball (2020)]], [[Kinio et al. (2019)]], [[Eukel, Frenzel, and Cernusca (2017)]], [[Cain (2019)]] and [[Adams et al. (2018)]]). Escape rooms offer students the opportunity to apply their acquired knowledge in a practical setting ([[Adams et al. (2018)]]). They not only motivate students to learn but also provide a safe, stress-reducing environment where students can test their knowledge ([[Mawhirter and Garofalo (2016)]]). Through immediate feedback, students can identify their strengths and weaknesses in a subject, thereby enhancing their understanding and skills ([[Mawhirter and Garofalo (2016)]]). Escape room activities require students to think outside the box with the knowledge they have learnt and to think about different solution strategies ([[Hermanns et al. (2017)]]). Students can memorise information better if they can apply it practically instead of just reading about it in a book ([[Hermanns et al. (2017)]]). Moreover, students learn trough escape rooms to work and make important decisions under time pressure ([[Rincón and Mejia (2020)]]). In another study students find escape rooms exciting because they never know what will come next ([[Roman et al. (2020)]]). Escape rooms enhance their critical thinking and promote collaboration with other students ([[Roman et al. (2020)]]). Within the given time, students can repeatedly attempt the tasks, allowing them to reflect on what they did wrong and why ([[Roman et al. (2020)]]).

Another study also confirms that escape room activities are a fun experience for students, increasing their motivation to learn, boosting student engagement, enhancing their knowledge, and proving to be an effective learning activity. Escape rooms also encourage students to review the material in advance. Students who prepared for the topic beforehand benefited the most from the escape room. The study shows statistically significant results, indicating that students who participated in the escape room had a higher pass rate than those who did not. After the escape room activity, many students were eager to learn the correct answers and understand the most effective ways to solve the puzzles ([[Lopez-Pernas et al. (2019b)]]). Escape rooms promote teamwork, leadership skills, creative thinking, problem-solving skills, student engagement and communication ([[Wu, Wagenschutz, and Hein (2018)]] and [[Lopez-Pernas et al. (2019a)]]). The study shows statistically significant results that the students have improved their knowledge through the escape room activity ([[Lopez-Pernas et al. (2019a)]]). In addition, students believe that the escape room is more beneficial to them than regular exercises ([[Lopez-Pernas et al. (2019a)]]). In a study the escape room activity has increased the number of correct answers and has boosted students' confidence in solving tasks accurately ([[Berthod et al. (2020)]]). Even after an extended period, students retained the knowledge needed to complete the tasks correctly ([[Berthod et al. (2020)]]). Students are more willing to tackle challenging concepts in a playful environment than in a traditional lecture setting ([[Berthod et al. (2020)]]).

However, if the escape room is poorly designed, it can also have negative consequences for students for instance due to time pressure, tasks that are too difficult or extrinsic factors such as grades, students may feel stressed during the escape room activity ([[Tercanli et al. (2021)]]). The difference between extrinsic and intrinsic motivation is that extrinsic motivation involves students being driven by external rewards such as grades, money, or punishments to participate in an activity. In contrast, intrinsic motivation involves students being driven by internal incentives such as personal interest, enjoyment, or curiosity to engage in an activity, regardless of external factors ([[Ryan and Deci (2000)]]). In a study, students felt frustrated due to insufficient time to finish all the tasks. These students mentioned that they were anxious and tried to find shorter ways to finish all the tasks within the given time, rather than truly understanding and learning the material. Furthermore, students were frustrated by unclear instructions and expressed a desire for more guidance and support during the activity ([[Hermanns et al. (2017)]]). Educational escape rooms do not always outperform traditional teaching methods such as lectures and reading texts because in a study by [[Waggener (1979)]], half of the surveyed students believed that reading texts provided them with a better understanding of principles and concepts compared to case studies or simulations. Additionally, lecture-based learning has its advantages over case-based or problem-based learning, including the ability to teach a large number of students simultaneously and requiring less effort and cost for professors ([[Ungaretti et al. (2015)]]).

## Overview of existing escape rooms at universities

Several studies have reported on the use of educational escape rooms in universities in a variety of disciplines such as in medicine and STEM (science, technology, engineering, and mathematics) education ([[Veldkamp et al. (2020)]]), but according to my research, there are fewer studies discussing the use of educational escape rooms in business and finance. This chapter presents four educational escape rooms, detailing their types, structures, contents, and processes, which have been implemented or are currently being used at various universities in the business and finance sectors. The first escape room focuses on corporate finance and financial accounting, the second on auditing, the third on managerial accounting, and the fourth on entrepreneurship. ## Usefulness of escape rooms at university level

Escape rooms offer an engaging and innovative way to enhance learning at the university level by promoting deeper understanding of course content, practical application of knowledge, and the development of essential soft skills.

**Engaging and effective learning:**
Escape rooms can be used to introduce a topic ([[Guckian, Sridhar, and Meggitt (2020)]] and [[Mac Gregor (2018)]]), to deepen the course content ([[Franco and DeLuca (2019)]]) or to review the course content at the end of the semester ([[Vergne, Simmons, and Bowen (2019)]] and [[Järveläinen and Paavilainen-Mäntymäki (2019)]]). While lectures, reading texts, and case studies are essential for students to learn and understand the material, simulations have proven to provide a superior practical learning and problem-solving experience ([[Waggener (1979)]] and [[Ungaretti et al. (2015)]]). When students are actively involved in the learning process then they can better retain and comprehend the material ([[Brady and Andersen (2021)]]). Learner-centred practices, like escape rooms, foster a deeper comprehension of concepts and offer a more enjoyable and effective way to prepare for exams than typical review sessions ([[Brady and Andersen (2021)]]). Escape room activities require students to think outside the box with the knowledge they have learnt and to think about different solution strategies ([[Hermanns et al. (2017)]]). Various subjects such as asset management, company valuation or market analysis could be integrated into educational escape rooms.

**Practical application of theoretical knowledge:**
Escape rooms offer students the opportunity to apply their acquired knowledge in a practical setting ([[Adams et al. (2018)]]). They not only motivate students to learn but also provide a safe environment where students can test their knowledge without the fear of consequences from making mistakes ([[Mawhirter and Garofalo (2016)]]). Within the given time, students can repeatedly attempt the tasks, allowing them to reflect on what they did wrong and why ([[Roman et al. (2020)]]). Students can memorise information better if they can apply it practically instead of just reading about it in a book ([[Hermanns et al. (2017)]]). Additionally, escape rooms can provide students with insight into how tasks might look in their future careers ([[Guckian, Sridhar, and Meggitt (2020)]]). A practical task could be, for instance, that students act as advisors and have to develop a suitable investment strategy based on the preferences and capabilities of a fictitious investor.

**Promoting soft skills:**
Nowadays, technical knowledge and routine skills alone are not sufficient to secure a job in the financial industry ([[AI Shayeb (2013)]]). Employers value interdisciplinary academic abilities and place a strong emphasis on soft skills ([[AI Shayeb (2013)]]). These include for instance communication, teamwork, creativity, problem-solving, time management, and a readiness to learn ([[AI Shayeb (2013)]] and [[Osmani et al. (2017)]]). Through escape rooms, students have the opportunity to practice efficient problem-solving, and the skills learned can often be transferred to other situations ([[Brady and Andersen (2021)]]). Escape rooms also encourage students to view problems from multiple perspectives, promote teamwork, enhance engagement and perseverance in task completion, and foster deeper understanding through team discussions ([[Fotaris and Mastoras (2019)]]). The need to collaborate as a team under time pressure enables students to improve their communication skills ([[Fotaris and Mastoras (2019)]]). Escape rooms promote teamwork, leadership skills, creative thinking, problem-solving skills, student engagement and communication ([[Wu, Wagenschutz, and Hein (2018)]] and [[Lopez-Pernas et al. (2019a)]]).

**Promoting digital skills:**
Because of the digital transformation of the financial services sector, the expansion of the mobile banking sector, and the fintech revolution, the knowledge and skills that are expected from employees are changing ([[Lavrinenko and Shmatko (2019)]]). By integrating digital tools and technologies relevant to the modern financial world into the escape room, students can enhance their digital skills, such as programming language proficiency, experience with banking software, and search optimization capabilities ([[Lavrinenko and Shmatko (2019)]]). These skills are highly sought after by employers in the financial sector today ([[Lavrinenko and Shmatko (2019)]]).

## Recommendations for lecturers

After discussing the usefulness of escape rooms in universities, here are some tips for lecturers who would like to create an educational escape room and integrate it into a lecture or seminar.

**Put together a team:**
It is recommended to design an escape room with an interdisciplinary team, as this approach provides a broader range of ideas and expertise ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). For instance, if you want to create an escape room for finance, the team could include the following members: one person with specific theoretical knowledge, who has a strong understanding of financial concepts and knows what knowledge is expected of students in the workplace; a professor who is familiar with the learning objectives and understands the best methods for teaching students, as well as where they typically struggle; another person with experience in developing games and puzzles, or who has even designed an escape room before; and finally, a few test participants, such as students ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Test participants are crucial for determining if the escape room functions as intended and for providing direct feedback, allowing for adjustments before it is used with a larger group of students ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]).

**Determine the target audience:**
Depending on the academic year and subject in which the escape room is used, its design must be adjusted accordingly ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). For example, if the escape room is intended for first-year students, it should be simpler since these students often lack prior knowledge ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Conversely, if the escape room is meant to be used towards the end of the degree program, the questions and puzzles can be more challenging ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). At this stage, students have already acquired a certain level of knowledge and the puzzles can be designed to encourage them to think outside the box to find solutions ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]).

**Determine the learning objectives:**
The selection of learning objectives is crucial, as not every objective is equally suitable for integration into an escape room ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Potential learning objectives might include enhancing cognitive and social skills such as teamwork, problem-solving, leadership and communication ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Other objectives could involve conveying or testing theoretical knowledge ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). By incorporating theory into the puzzles, one can teach, reinforce, or test the material in a way that would be difficult with traditional lectures ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Additionally, other learning goals might focus on students acquiring technical skills through simulations, which they will later use in the workplace ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]).

**Determine the modality:**
The choice of modality depends on the available resources and costs ([[Barnes and Enget (2022)]] and [[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). One must decide whether to create the escape room in a physical space with items for example boxes and locks, or virtually using a suitable program ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Physical escape rooms can provide an exciting experience as students can touch objects and work with their hands, allowing for in-person discussions, but they are more expensive due to the required materials and rooms, and it is challenging to conduct them with many students at the same time ([[Barnes and Enget (2022)]] and [[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Digital escape rooms are more flexible, allowing students to solve them from home and enabling multiple students to participate at the same time, and they are often less expensive compared to physical escape rooms, but they lack the sensory experience and may encounter technical problems ([[Barnes and Enget (2022)]] and [[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Therefore, digital escape rooms would be more suitable for lectures and physical or digital escape rooms for seminars.

**Determine the duration:**
Before the actual escape room activity, the instructor should take at least 10 minutes to explain to the students the purpose of the escape room, its learning objectives, and the rules ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). A good introduction also reduces students' uncertainty ([[Hermanns et al. (2017)]]). A recommended time limit for the escape room itself is 45 minutes, ideally between 30 and 60 minutes ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). The duration should be long enough to achieve the learning objectives but not so long that it becomes exhausting ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). After the escape room activity, the instructor should take 30 to 45 minutes to give the students feedback on their performance, discuss the solutions and address any questions with the students ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Debriefing sessions are important because discussing and sharing what was learned help students to improve memory and enhances understanding ([[Fotaris and Mastoras (2022)]]).

**Determine the number of participants:**
The number of participants depends on the students and their knowledge, the learning objectives, and resources such as space and necessary materials ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). It also depends on whether the escape room activity is to be done individually or in groups ([[Clarke et al. (2017)]]). It is recommended to have 4 to 8 students per session ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). If participants are divided into teams, 3 to 5 students per team is ideal ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). The more participants there are, the more ideas, skills, and experiences are exchanged; however, it becomes more challenging for the instructor to maintain control and oversight ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). It is therefore advisable to have several supervisors so that participants can be better assisted if necessary ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). With fewer participants, students have more opportunities to actively engage and contribute to the solution ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]).

**Choose a theme and a narrative:**
Before designing an escape room, one must choose a theme and create a storyline ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). A compelling and engaging story is crucial to keep participants motivated from start to finish ([[Clarke et al. (2017)]]). It provides a clear narrative thread and enhances the understanding of how the individual puzzles connect ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). The story can be realistic for instance, if the tasks are related to banking, the scenario could be set in a bank, with participants acting as customer advisors ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). This approach gives students a direct practical link to the escape room ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). However, the escape room’s story can also diverge from the tasks and be more creative, such as a jungle expedition, a space mission or a murder mystery ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). This makes the activity feel less strict and more enjoyable for students as they solve the puzzles ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). However, it is important to ensure that all students can relate to the theme ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Avoid culturally specific themes that only those who grew up in that culture can understand ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Additionally, consider that puzzles and idioms that work in one language may not necessarily translate well into another ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]).

**Create the puzzles:**
The puzzles should not be too difficult, otherwise it will take far too long to solve them, but they should also not be too simple and obvious, otherwise it will be too boring, and the learning effect will be low ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). It is recommended to include a variety of puzzle types in an escape room to keep participants engaged and to encourage different ways of thinking (Fides-Valero et al. (2024)). Puzzles can involve searching for something, performing calculations, discovering solution words, recognizing patterns, and more ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Additionally, ensure that answers based on spelling are unambiguous ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). It is also important to incorporate appropriate hints into the puzzles, as hints can be very helpful if participants get stuck, without giving away the solution ([[Nicholson (2015)]]). Furthermore, you need to decide whether the puzzles should be solved sequentially, simultaneously, or a combination of both ([[Nicholson (2015)]]).

**Test the escape room:**
In the final step, you need to test the escape room prototype to ensure that the puzzles are comprehensible, can be solved within the planned time and are free of errors ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). To test the prototype, you can use people from the target audience, but this is not mandatory ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). The testers simply need to be individuals unfamiliar with the escape room ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). It is crucial to gather feedback from these testers, identifying what worked well and what did not, so that necessary improvements can be made ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]). Once the prototype functions well, you can finalize the escape room and test with a larger audience ([[Fides-Valero et al. (2024)]]).
