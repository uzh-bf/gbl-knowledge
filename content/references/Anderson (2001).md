
## Summary

This is an extensive revision of the original Bloom's Taxonomy, which was a framework for categorizing educational goals, objectives, and standards. The revised taxonomy restructures the categories from the original cognitive domain into a more dynamic continuum that ranges from lower-order to higher-order thinking skills. It emphasizes the interrelation between knowledge types (factual, conceptual, procedural, and metacognitive) and cognitive processes such as remembering, understanding, applying, analyzing, evaluating, and creating. This revision aims to provide educators with a more detailed structure for designing, implementing, and assessing learning experiences that foster complex thinking and deeper understanding.

Anderson, L. W. (2001). A taxonomy for learning, teaching, and assessing: a revision of Bloom’s taxonomy of educational objectives (1. ed). Longman.
